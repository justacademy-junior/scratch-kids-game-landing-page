Your 30 minute game plan

Four small stages. Each one adds one new idea, so nothing feels too big.

Minutes 0 to 8🎨

Set the scene

Pick a backdrop, delete the orange cat and add a basket sprite. Kids choose the look, so the game is theirs from the start.

Minutes 8 to 13🧺

Move the basket

A forever block checks the arrow keys again and again, so the basket slides left and right. First real code, first big smile.

Minutes 13 to 20🍎

Drop the apples

Apples start at a random spot at the top and fall down in a loop. Variables called score and lives keep track of the game.

Minutes 20 to 30🏆

Score and game over

If the apple touches the basket, add a point and play a pop. Otherwise lose a life. At zero lives, say Game Over and stop.

Questions parents ask

Is Scratch really free?

Yes. The Scratch website and its offline editor are free to use.

What age is best for Scratch?

Scratch 3.0 suits roughly ages eight to sixteen. ScratchJr, a tablet app, suits ages five to seven.

Does my child need to type or know English well?

Basic reading helps, but nearly everything is drag and drop. Typing is limited to short names and numbers.

Can my child learn without a teacher?

Yes, tutorials and beginner projects are everywhere. A teacher can speed things up and keep motivation high, but it is not required.

How long does a first game take?

Thirty minutes to an hour. Take longer if your child wants to design their own characters.

Ready to press the green flag?

Sit beside your child, let them hold the mouse, and see what they build.
